How the rhythm is made

Every bit of visible line in a patch votes for its direction. If most lines run the same way, alignment is near 1 and the two pulses in each beat are evenly spaced. If they run every which way, alignment is near 0 and the second pulse comes late: short–long.

Rule: the second pulse lands at 0.75 − 0.25 × alignment of the beat. After you answer, the big numerals take a font weight from the alignment: thin means swirly, heavy means lined up. The weight scale is stretched across this image's measured range, so the least lined-up patch found is weight 200 and the most lined-up is weight 800.

The numbers here are a quick practice measurement made in your browser, not the team's checked numbers. The rule is a choice the team made. It describes the picture, not water speed, turbulence or ocean health.
